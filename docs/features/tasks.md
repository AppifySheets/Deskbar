The bottom of the bar carries a short task list, for the things you want to remember while you work.

## How to use it

At the top of the list is a text box that says where a note would go, "Add to Deskbar…" (or "Add…" on a desktop you have not named). Under it are the first three open tasks, one per line, with the workspace each belongs to as a small chip in that workspace's colour (or "none"). How long ago a task was added is in the [tasks panel](#the-tasks-window), on every line, and in the bar line's tooltip as its date. The title gets all the room the line has left, and a title too long for one line takes a second, ending in … if even two are not enough (the tooltip has it whole); on a narrow bar, where the chip would leave the title too little, the chip shrinks to a dot in the workspace's colour, with its name in the tooltip. Tasks that wait on you come first, then the rest in the order of [importance and urgency](#important-and-urgent), each group in the order you drag them into. On a bar docked to a screen edge, the list uses all the height the workspace rows leave free, so it shows as many tasks as fit, never fewer than three. The rest are not cut off: scroll the list with the mouse wheel to reach them.

When more than three are open, a small "+2 more" at the right end of the row under the box, after the workspace chip and the two toggles, counts the ones out of sight, scrolled past or still below, and the count follows as you scroll. On a narrow bar it shortens to "+2" (and "All tasks" to "All"), with the whole words in its tooltip, rather than wrapping. It turns orange and reads "+2 waiting" when the hidden ones are waiting on you, so nothing that needs you stays hidden without a sign. With nothing out of sight it reads **All tasks**. Either way, a click opens the [tasks panel](#the-tasks-window), where every task is, open and closed.

Next to it, at the very end of the row, is **Manager**. It opens the [manager session](claude-sessions.md#the-manager-session): one background Claude a day that keeps an eye on the whole board for you, comments on tasks and passes your answers on, but never closes, deletes or finishes a task.

Click in the box to write a note. Under it, always, is one chip, the workspace the note goes to, with a small arrow, like "Deskbar ▾". It starts as the workspace you are on (and follows you while the box is empty), or you can make it **none**. Click it and a menu opens with **none** first and then every workspace, each one its chip in its own colour, in the bar's order, with grouped workspaces under their group's name; the one chosen is ticked. Above all of them is a **Recent** row: the workspaces your newest tasks went to, newest first, up to five, so the few you use are a press or two away with the keyboard. They also stay in their usual places below. Every workspace menu of the task list has this row, the line's workspace chip and the panel's Move to as well. Pick one and the chip changes to it. The box names where the note will go ("Add to Deskbar..." or, with none chosen, "Add..."). On a narrow bar the row wraps onto a second line. Clicking the chip starts a note just as clicking in the box does. Beside the chip are the two toggles a task line has, important and urgent: turn on neither, either or both before you press Enter, and the note goes to the top of the quadrant they give. They are off again after each note. Press Enter and the workspace menu opens under the box before anything is added, with the keyboard on the workspace the note would go to, so a note typed on the wrong desktop does not land there unseen. Press Enter again to add it there and start Claude on it (see below). To put it somewhere else, move with the arrows or Tab, or type the first letters of a workspace's name to jump to it, then press Enter (or click a chip). Esc closes the menu and leaves the text in the box, with nothing added. Ctrl+Enter asks the same way and only adds the note, without Claude. Esc in the box clears it. Shift+Enter starts a new line, and a long note wraps instead of running out of sight: the box grows with what you type, up to six lines, and scrolls after that. The first line becomes the task's title and the lines after it its description. Either way the keyboard goes back to the window you were typing in, as long as that window is on the desktop you are looking at, because Deskbar never switches desktops to hand it back. If you click into another app while typing, what you typed stays in the box, with its chip, until you come back to it. Enter hands the note to Claude as it adds it: the note is added and Claude starts on it in the background, in the workspace's one folder or one you pick. Where Claude has nowhere to start (a workspace with no repo, Claude sessions turned off in Manage, or Claude Code not installed), Enter only adds the note, so a personal note does not ask for a folder. Ctrl+Enter always only adds; that note is selected and glows so you can see where it landed. The play button after the important and urgent toggles starts Claude as Enter does, and on a workspace with no folder it asks for one. No terminal opens, you stay on the workspace and the thread you were on, and a small "Claude started" card shows for a few seconds in the bottom-right corner of the screen without taking focus; click it to open the task in the tasks panel, never a terminal. In the Inbox view that is the task's page, where Claude's messages come in as it works; in the List view the task's thread unfolds. If the start fails you are told at once. The camera right after the important and urgent toggles makes a task from the window you were working in and starts Claude on it, in one press: it takes a picture of that window (the window itself, not the bar or anything lying over it), adds a task with the picture and a line naming the window by its title and program, and starts Claude as the play button does. Anything you typed in the box becomes the task's title; with nothing typed the task is called "Picture" until the session renames it after reading. With no window to take, such as on an empty desktop, Deskbar says so and adds nothing.

To run one of a repo's own Claude Code skills on a workspace, type `/` at the start of the box. A list under it shows the skills the workspace's repos keep in their `.claude/skills`, and narrows as you type the name: `/ref` finds `/refresh-pr`. Your user-wide skills, your plugins' and Claude Code's built-in ones are left out. The list comes from Claude Code itself, which Deskbar asks in the workspace's folders and in your home folder: what it lists in a repo and not at home is that repo's own. A skill only some of the workspace's repos have shows their names beside it. The first `/` in a workspace takes a few seconds while Claude Code answers, and after that the list is instant. Down and Up move through it, Tab or Enter puts the skill in the box with a space after it for anything you want to add, and Esc closes the list. Enter, or the play button, then adds the task and starts Claude, and the session's first message runs the skill (Ctrl+Enter while the list is open takes the skill you are on and adds the task without starting Claude). A task whose title starts with a repo's own skill starts in a repo that has it. The tasks panel's **+ Add** box has the same list.

The box takes pictures too. Press Ctrl+V with a screenshot on the clipboard (Win+Shift+S works well), or drop image files on the box, and each picture waits under it as a small thumbnail with an **x** that takes it out again. Enter adds the task with what you typed as its title and the pictures as its description. With nothing typed, the task is called "Picture" ("Pictures" for more than one). Esc drops the pictures along with the text; clicking into another app keeps them, as it keeps the text.

A line on the bar has no tick box: its title starts at the left edge, and a title too long for one row goes on in a second row that runs the whole width of the line, under the workspace chip too. To close a task from the bar, right-click its line and pick **Close**, or click the line and close it in the [tasks panel](#the-tasks-window). Closed from the bar, the line stays where it is for six seconds, struck through, with **Undo** at its start. Undo brings the task back, and otherwise the line then leaves. Past the six seconds, `task list --closed` finds a closed note and `task reopen <id>` brings it back.

Point at a line for its tooltip: its status and title, its question or status line, its quadrant, its workspace and, when it belongs to one, its [goal](goals.md).

The workspace chip at the end of a line is a button. Click it to move the task: a menu lists **none** and every workspace in its colour, with the task's own ticked. The title's first row stops short of it and its second row runs on underneath, so the chip is never cut off; on a narrow bar, where the chip would leave the title too little, the chip becomes a dot in the workspace's colour, and clicking the dot opens the same menu.

Right-click a line for its menu: **Edit** first, which opens the title for editing right in the line (Enter saves, Shift+Enter adds a line break, Esc cancels), then one Claude item: on a Claude task **Open Claude**, which goes to its session the way a double-click does but without opening the task in the tasks panel first, and on any other task **Start Claude on this**, which hands the task to a new background Claude session (see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md#going-to-a-session-and-starting-one)), then **Add subtask…**, the two flags, **Move to workspace...** and **Close**. Click anywhere else on a line to open that task in the [tasks panel](#the-tasks-window): the window comes forward and takes the foreground, with the task selected, scrolled into view and expanded to its comments, and you stay where you are. Double-click the line to go to its workspace and, for a Claude task, to its session: its terminal or VS Code window comes to the front, opening on the session or resuming it first when needed. A note on no workspace has nowhere to go, so the double-click does nothing, and a double-click goes only to the line its first click landed on. The task you clicked last stays highlighted, with a lighter background and a thin blue edge on its left, on the bar and in the tasks panel alike, until you pick another or the task is closed. Bringing a Claude task's terminal window to the front picks its task the same way (see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md)). You can close a Claude task yourself whenever you like, whatever its status, with **Close** in its menu on the bar or its tick box in the tasks panel. While Claude is still working on it (it is not done yet and its session is still running), Close does not close it straight away. A small menu says Claude is still working on the task and offers **Stop Claude and close**, which closes the task and stops its background session; the conversation is kept, so double-clicking the task later resumes it. A session in VS Code or a terminal cannot be stopped from Deskbar: its window stays open, and its next message tells it the task is closed. A task that is done waits for you to look at it and close it, and closes at once. A task that is not finished yet, because it still asks you something or a Claude session is still on it, and whose work is not merged, does not close on one click either: a small menu says what is still open and offers **Close anyway**, and Esc or a click elsewhere leaves the task as it was. Once its pull request has merged, such a task closes at once, for example one waiting for you to install the update that carries the change. Claude's turn is over there, so there is nothing to stop; Deskbar stops its background session a little later, once the task is closed. Closing has the same six seconds of Undo, and Undo puts the task back exactly as it was: its status, its question if it was waiting on you, its place and the selection. A Claude task that was still in progress (ticked in the tasks panel, where it shows) comes back to the tasks panel, not to the bar, since the bar lists a Claude task only while it needs you.

A task is in one of five statuses: backlog (a plain note), in progress, waiting on you, done or closed. Closed tasks leave the list. A task that is done has a green tick in front of its title, on the bar and in the tasks panel, so finished work waiting for you to close it does not look like work still going on.

## Important and urgent

Every task can be marked important, urgent, both or neither. The flags show in the title itself, on the bar and in the tasks panel's List and Matrix: an important task's title is **bold** and an urgent task's is *italic*, so a task that is both is bold italic and a task with neither is plain. On the bar that is all there is, with no sign beside the title: right-click a line and pick **Important** or **Urgent** (ticked while it is on) to change them. In the tasks panel each line also has two small toggles, an exclamation mark for important and a clock for urgent. Off, they stay out of sight until you point at the line or select it, except in a narrow panel, where the buttons have a row of their own under the title and the toggles stay in view in grey all the time; on, they show in colour, red for important and amber for urgent. Hover one to see its name.

The two flags sort the list into the four quadrants of Stephen Covey's time management matrix, from *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*:

- I: urgent and important
- II: important, not urgent
- III: urgent, not important
- IV: neither

Tasks waiting on you stay on top whatever their flags, and the tasks that are done, waiting for you to look and close them, sit right under the questions. A new question goes to the top of the questions and a newly done task to the top of the done ones; after that they stay in the order you drag them into. Below them come quadrants I, II, III and IV, with a little space between groups. A new task goes to the top of its quadrant (a new task has no flags, so that is quadrant IV). Setting or clearing a flag moves the task to the top of the quadrant it lands in. A task that is done keeps its place in its quadrant for when it goes back to work; a closed task leaves, and one you reopen comes back at the top of its quadrant. Undo after closing puts the task back where it was. A status line from a session never moves a line.

Within a quadrant the order is yours. Drag a line up or down and let go: it goes just above the line you let go over if you are on its top half, just below it if you are on its bottom half. Let go among another quadrant's lines and the task takes that quadrant's flags. A press that does not move is still a click. Tasks waiting on you and done ones are dragged the same way, but only among their own kind: a question among the questions, a done task among the done ones. They stay on top until answered or closed, so let go above or below them and the task goes to the top or the end of its kind. Nothing else can be dropped above them. Once you answer, or a done task goes back to work, the task drops into its quadrant at its place. In the tasks panel, the tasks in the **Claude working** block drag like any line of their quadrant (the block keeps quadrant order, so dragging one among the others reorders its quadrant), and once the session ends a task goes back to its quadrant at its place. The list does not redraw while you hold the mouse button down, so nothing moves under the pointer.

## The tasks window

"+2 more" (or **All tasks**), at the right end of the row under the box, opens the tasks panel: a full-height strip docked just inside the bar, with every task. It is registered with the shell after the bar, so the bar stays the outer strip and maximized windows start beside both. It is on every virtual desktop, always on top, and not in Alt+Tab, on the taskbar or tiled. Click the same button again, or the x in its toolbar, to close it (`deskbar.cmd tasks-window show` and `hide` do the same). Drag its inner edge to set the width: 460 DIP to start, 360 at the least, half the screen at the most. Deskbar remembers the width and whether the panel was open, so it comes back after a restart. A floating bar still gets the panel, on its nearer screen edge; minimizing the bar takes the panel away and restoring it brings it back. A full-screen app makes the panel step out of the topmost band until you leave it.

The search box at the top (Ctrl+F; Esc or the × at its right end clears it) keeps the tasks that match every word you type, in the title, status line or question. Type a task's id, or its first few characters (the eight that sessions and comments name it by, like 173ef268), and the panel shows that task wherever it is, closed and snoozed ones included, whatever the other filters say. The **Claude | working | idle** switch keeps only tasks with a live Claude session, only those whose session is working right now (their session squares glow), or only the idle ones, whose session has finished its turn without asking you anything. Click the part that is on to turn the switch off. Like **Current workspace**, it stays on when the panel closes and after a restart. A status chip, checkbox or view that is on is filled blue. Below 850 DIP the lines are slim: the status chip and the assignee move into the tooltip, with the age again ("Done · 5h · Claude"; a done task keeps its green tick on the line, and the age stays on it too) and Close and Reopen into the ⋯ menu. On a slim line the title has the whole width, and the line's squares, age, workspace chip and buttons sit on a row of their own under it, starting at the title's left edge, with a question or status line under them. The List and Matrix switch shows from 780 DIP. The search and the other filters start clear each time the panel opens.

The panel opens on the list itself, not on a form. Open tasks are grouped as on the bar: **Waiting on you** first, then **Claude working**, the tasks a Claude session is live on that are neither waiting on you nor done (in quadrant order, each quadrant in your order): first the ones whose session is working right now, then, dimmer and under a dashed line captioned "idle", the ones whose session has finished its turn. A session that only waits on its own background job, such as a pull request watch, counts as idle. A line moves to idle about half a minute after its turn ends. Then come the four quadrants under Covey's own words, "I Urgent and important", "II Important, not urgent", "III Urgent, not important" and "IV Not urgent, not important". The chips along the top filter by status and show how many tasks each one holds: Open, Waiting on you, Done, In progress, Backlog and **Closed**. A status chip keeps the quadrants and shows only that status in each. Closed tasks only show under their own chip, the most recently closed first, each with **Reopen**. **Workspace: all** narrows the list to one workspace, or to tasks on none. Tick **Current workspace** beside it and the list follows you: switch desktops and it shows the tasks of the workspace you are on now (on a desktop you never named, the tasks on no workspace). Untick it to see every workspace again. Unlike the other filters, it stays ticked when the panel closes and after a restart. **Assigned: all** narrows it to the tasks assigned to you (**me**) or to **Claude**. Once you have a [goal](goals.md), **Goal: all** next to it narrows the list and the Matrix to one goal's tasks, or to tasks with no goal.

Each line has the two toggles, and drags the same way as on the bar: within its group to reorder, into another quadrant to take its flags. A long title wraps onto more lines rather than being cut short. To edit a task in place, select its line and press F2, or choose **Edit** in its menu: the title and its description open together in two boxes (Tab moves between them), Enter saves both, Shift+Enter adds a line break, and Esc or a click elsewhere cancels. The description is yours: markdown you write under the title, for any task, shown on its line as a second block and on the bar as a first line where a Claude task shows its status line. A Claude task's status line stays the session's and is not editable. The thread reads like a chat: your comments are blue bubbles on the right, a Claude session's (or the manager's) are bubbles on the left, each with its time on the small line under it and its author named there too ("you · 14:28"); several bubbles in a row from one author carry one line, "you · 19:03", under the last (a bubble the next one follows more than five minutes later keeps its own time, and pointing at a bubble with no line shows its time beside it), and Deskbar's own lines, such as "Resumed.", are small centred lines. A date line ("Today", "25 Nov") heads each day's comments, so a bubble shows only its time of day, and you post with the round arrow at the end of the comment box (or Enter). Point at a comment to see its buttons. Each of your own comments has **Edit** beside its time, which rewrites it in place (Enter saves, Esc keeps it); a comment Claude wrote cannot be edited. A comment can be **settled**, with the **Settle** button beside its time, once its question has its answer: it is kept, but folds away behind one dim line at the top of the thread, "2 settled comments", which unfolds them on a click (and **Unsettle** brings one back). When a session settles its question with `task answered`, its comments written since it asked are settled for you, and a session can settle one of its own with `task comment-settle`. The bar shows a title's first line, wrapped onto two lines at most, with the whole title in its tooltip.

**List** and **Matrix** at the top right switch between this list and Covey's matrix itself: a 2 by 2 grid with urgent and not urgent across the top and important and not important down the side, so quadrant I sits top left and quadrant II top right. Each cell lists its quadrant's open tasks in their order, with the same lines, and scrolls on its own when it is long. Tasks waiting on you sit at the top of their own quadrant's cell, in orange, with the done ones under them, and the ones Claude is working on come next. Drag a line within a cell to reorder it, or into another cell, an empty one included, to give it that cell's flags. A question or a done task moves among its own kind in its cell, and dropped on another cell it takes that cell's flags and sits at the top of it. Quadrant II, important but not urgent, is drawn a little brighter, since it is the one Covey says to put first. The window remembers which view you left it on.

Each line shows the task's title, then its question or status line under it, then its status in a small chip (green **Done**, orange **Waiting**, blue **In progress**, grey **Backlog**), then its sessions (a square for each, blue while it is live, with "2 sessions, 1 live" or "1 session, ended"), its workspace and its comment count. A Claude task is marked by the orange edge at the start of its line, as on the bar. Every open line, a note or a Claude task, a done one included, has a tick box that closes it, with the same six seconds of Undo as on the bar. Each part of a line (flags, status, sessions, who it is assigned to, age, workspace, comments, the ⋯ button) sits in a column of its own, the same on every line, flush with the panel's right edge, and every line spans the whole panel, so a missing part leaves a gap instead of moving its neighbours. **Close** in a line's ⋯ menu closes any open task. On a task Claude is still working on, the tick and **Close** both offer **Stop Claude and close** first, as on the bar; on a task that still asks you something, or has a session on it, and whose work is not merged, they offer **Close anyway**.

### Buttons on a line

The menu's actions are on the line too, as small icon buttons just before the ⋯, as many as the line has room for, in this order: **Edit**, **Open in window**, **Add subtask**, **Assign to**, **Related to**, **Move to** and **Delete**. The panel at its narrowest has room for **Edit** and perhaps **Open in window**, the default width for five or six, depending on the line's comment count, a panel about 520 DIP wide for all seven, and a full line (from 850 DIP) has all seven at its right end from about 940 DIP. Narrower, a full line keeps its title readable first: the buttons give way, down to the ⋯ alone. **Assign to** and **Move to** open their choices under the button, and so does a click on the line's workspace chip, which offers the same move, and **Delete** opens **Delete for good**, so it still takes a second click. Each button keeps its place on every line, and an action that does not apply (a closed task cannot be edited) leaves its place empty. The ⋯ menu keeps every item. A note's line has a play button where a Claude task has **Open Claude**: **Start Claude on this**. Like the ⋯, the buttons are dim until the pointer is on the line.

- Click a line to select it and open its comments; click it again to close them, or press Esc with it selected. A click goes nowhere. Double-click it to go to its workspace (a Claude task's session window): the tasks panel stays open. A Claude task's line has an **Open Claude** button, the open-in-window icon next to its comments, which goes to its session at once without opening the task. The same on the bar: a click on a task opens it in the tasks panel, selected, scrolled into view and expanded to its comments, and a double-click takes you to its workspace and window. The line stays highlighted, as on the bar, and when the window opens it scrolls to that task.
- Click the comment glyph (with the count beside it when there are comments) to open the task's comments. Each comment shows who wrote it, **Claude** or **you**, and when, and its text is formatted: bold, italics, code, code blocks, links and lists. A web link opens in your default browser, in the task's workspace: when the task belongs to another workspace, Deskbar switches there first, so the page opens beside that task's windows and not on the desktop you were on. A task on no workspace, or one in the workspace you are on, opens its links where you are. This holds for every link in a task, in the tasks panel and in the task's own window. A link to a file on this PC (`[the page](file:///C:/work/index.html)`) can be clicked too, in comments, descriptions, questions and status lines: a page, picture, PDF or text file opens in its usual program, a folder opens in Explorer, and any other file is only shown in Explorer, never started. Links to network shares stay plain text. Type in the box under the comments and press Enter to add one as **you**. Shift+Enter starts a new line.
- Pictures go in a comment too. Take a screenshot (Win+Shift+S or Print Screen) or copy an image file in Explorer, click in the comment box and press Ctrl+V, or drop image files on the box. Each picture waits under the box as a small thumbnail with an **x** that takes it out again; Ctrl+V with only text on the clipboard pastes the text as usual. Enter posts the words and the pictures together, and a comment can be pictures alone. In the thread a comment's pictures show as thumbnails under its text (they always show, even when a long text is folded to six lines). They are small, so a screenshot does not take over the conversation. Click one and it opens large over the screen you are on, with the rest dimmed behind it; click anywhere or press Esc to close it, or press **Open in viewer** to open the file in your image viewer. The description takes pictures the same way: in the editor, paste or drop them into the description's box, and they wait under it with the ones it already has, each with its **x**; Enter saves them all and Esc keeps none of the new ones. An open line shows its description's pictures under the text, and a closed line whose description is only pictures says "image".
- The pictures are saved when the comment is posted or the description saved, never before, as files in `%APPDATA%\Deskbar\task-images`, one folder per task. A picture wider than 4096 pixels is scaled down to 4096. Closing a task keeps its pictures; deleting it deletes them. A Claude session sees each picture's full path in `task list` and reads it, and Deskbar adds one rule to Claude Code's settings so a session can read that folder without asking you first (see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md)).
- A comment you post on a Claude task goes to its session: an idle session wakes with it within a few seconds, a busy one gets it at its next step, and one that is not running is resumed with it. See [Your comments wake the session](claude-sessions.md#your-comments-wake-the-session).
- To have the session run one of its repo's own Claude Code skills, type `/` at the start of the comment box. The same list as the add box's opens under it, with the skills of the repo the task's session works in, and narrows as you type. A task with no Claude session yet lists none, since its comments reach no session. Tab or Enter puts the skill in the box with a space after it for anything you want to add, and the next Enter posts the comment. The session is then asked to run that skill, with the rest of your comment as what it works on. A comment that only looks like a skill, such as `/etc/hosts is wrong`, or names a skill that repo does not have, goes to the session as plain words. The task's own window has the same list, where Ctrl+Enter posts.
- A thread keeps itself short. A comment shows whole, however many lines it takes, up to about fifteen; a longer one shows its first six lines and ends in **Show more**, which opens it in place (**Show less** folds it back). When a thread has more than three comments it shows the latest three under a dim "3 earlier comments" line that unfolds the rest; settled comments fold away separately and do not count. The panel remembers what you unfolded for as long as it is open, through every redraw.
- The list holds still while you are in a thread. With a task's comments open, and while you are using the panel, no line moves: a task that a session closes, marks done or sends to Waiting on you keeps its place, drawn as it is now (closed, with its new question), and a task a session adds waits. What a line says still updates as it happens. The list rearranges once you close the comments, or once you have left the panel alone for about a minute. A note you add, a task you click on the bar and a filter you change show at once, and a task you close yourself still leaves after its six seconds of Undo.
- To see more or less of a long comment without opening it all, drag the thin band under its text: down shows more, up shows less, down to a single line. The height you leave it at stays while Deskbar runs. The same band sits under an open task's description and question.
- Right-click a line, or click **...** at its end, for its menu. **Edit** comes first and opens the title for editing in place. The rest of the menu sets the status by hand (Backlog, In progress, Waiting on you or Done), closes or reopens the task, hands it over with **Assign to** (**Me** or **Claude**, the current one ticked), moves it to another workspace or deletes it. Delete needs a second click, on **Delete for good**, because it cannot be undone. Once you have a goal, it also has **Goal**: **None**, then this month's and this week's open goals, with the task's own ticked. Linking a task to a goal changes nothing else about it.
- **+ Add** opens a box for a note. Beside it are the important and urgent toggles, then the chip saying which workspace it goes on, which you can change. Enter opens the same workspace menu as the bar's box, with the keyboard on the chip's workspace; Enter again adds the note there at the top of the quadrant the toggles give, the arrows or a name's first letters pick another, and Esc goes back to the text. Esc in the box closes it. Enter also starts Claude on the note, and Ctrl+Enter only adds it, as in the bar's box. The play button after the toggles adds the note and starts Claude on it in the background, as Enter does: no terminal, the panel stays where it was, and the "Claude started" card confirms the start. The camera before the play button makes a task from the window you were in before you clicked the panel, as the bar's camera does; with no window to take, the panel's problem line says so. As in the bar's box, Shift+Enter starts a new line, the box grows with the text up to six lines, and the first line is the title with the rest the description. In the List the toggles start off and are off again after each note. In the Matrix they follow the active cell, which has a blue edge: the cell you last clicked in, or the one holding the task you selected, and quadrant II until then. Click in a cell and the toggles take its flags; flip a toggle and the active cell moves to match. A note added there lands at the top of the active cell.
- The add box takes pictures as a comment box does: Ctrl+V a screenshot or drop image files on it, and each waits under the box with its **x**. Enter adds the task with what you typed as its title and the pictures as its description. A task can be pictures alone: with nothing typed it is called "Picture" ("Pictures" for more than one), and you can rename it later. Esc closes the box and drops the waiting pictures. The add box at the top of the bar's task list takes pictures the same way.

## A task in its own window

The panel is narrow on purpose, which makes a long task hard to read and harder to write. **Open in window**, second on a task's menu in the panel and on the bar (after **Edit**), and the first button in its open thread, opens the task in an ordinary window: a title bar, resizable and maximizable, on the taskbar and in Alt+Tab. Double-click still goes to the task's workspace, as before. An open line in the panel shows its question and its description to four lines each, so one long task cannot fill the panel; when either runs longer, **Show all in window** under them opens this window, where both show in full.

- The header has the title, the status (with **Undo** while a close can still be undone), the workspace (click it to move the task), who it is assigned to, the **Important** and **Urgent** toggles and its age, then the question or status line in full, with its links clickable. **Edit** and **⋯** are on the right; **⋯** has the status, close or reopen, **Assign to**, **Move to…**, **Go to its workspace**, **Show in the tasks panel** and **Delete**.
- The description is rendered at full width with its pictures. Under it is the whole thread: every comment, none folded behind "N earlier comments" and none cut to three lines. Settled comments still fold behind their own line. The box at the end posts a comment as you with **Ctrl+Enter**; a plain Enter is a new line, and pictures paste or drop in as they do in the panel. What you type stays in the box while the task changes around it.
- On a window at least 1100 DIP wide, a column on the right holds the Claude sessions (each with **Open**), **Start Claude on this** while none of them is running, the subtasks (each opens in a window of its own) and the details: quadrant, workspace, assignee, goal, parent, when it was added and its id, which you can select and copy. A narrower window puts the column under the thread.
- **Edit**, F2 or Ctrl+E turns the title and description into large boxes, with **Markdown** and **Preview** tabs. Pictures paste or drop into the description as in the panel's editor. **Ctrl+S** or **Ctrl+Enter** saves both and **Esc** keeps nothing. Your own comments have **Edit** in place (Ctrl+Enter saves, Esc keeps it).
- Each task has one window: asking again brings it to the front. It always comes to the desktop you are on, moved there if you left it on another. Every task window opens where you last moved, resized or closed one, maximized if that one was, and a second one open at the same time opens a little down and to the right. Deleting the task closes its window, and the windows close with Deskbar.
- On a tiled workspace a task window is a tile like any other app's window, not a window floating over the grid. Where the grid puts it is not remembered as the place task windows open at; only a place you gave one on an untiled workspace is.
- `deskbar.cmd task open <id>` does the same from a script.

**While the [Inbox](#the-inbox) is the chosen view,** the window shows the same page the panel shows for the task, in the Inbox's dark look, in a column at most 880 DIP wide. The title bar, the place, the size, one window per task and everything above about opening and closing the window stay as they are. Switching to List or Matrix, or back to the Inbox, changes every open window at once; what you typed in the comment box goes with it, an open editor closes without saving, and pictures waiting under the box are dropped. Stats laid over the Inbox changes nothing in a window.

- The top row has the state mark, the title and **⋯**, with no back arrow. Under it come the state in words, the workspace chip (click it to move the task), the task's pull requests, **Undo** while a close can still be undone, and the **Important** and **Urgent** toggles. Then the description in full with its pictures, the question in a bubble (or the status line), and the whole thread: every comment, none folded behind "N earlier comments", settled ones dimmed in their places, and none cut short. The comment box is at the bottom edge, with **Open Claude** (or **Start Claude on this**) at its left, as on the panel's page. The window opens with the keyboard in it, ready to type.
- A subtask, the parent and a linked task each open in a window of their own. Esc does nothing in a window, since there is no list behind it.
- **⋯** is the panel's menu for the task, with **Show in the tasks panel** in place of **Open in window**.
- The sessions, the subtasks and the task's pull requests are in the page itself, so the right-hand column of the window is gone, with its **Details** (quadrant, goal, when it was added, the id) and the age. **Copy link** on **⋯** has the id.
- **Editing and keys are the panel's, not the old window's.** Edit (or F2) opens the panel's in-place boxes for the title and the description: Enter saves, Shift+Enter is a new line, Esc keeps nothing. The old window's Markdown and Preview tabs, Ctrl+E, Ctrl+S and Ctrl+Enter to save are gone. In the comment box Enter posts and Shift+Enter is a new line (the old window posted with Ctrl+Enter, and Enter was a new line).

## Links to a task

**Copy link** on a task's menu, in the tasks panel and on the bar, copies a link to the task as markdown: `[Deskbar task 5016d533](https://deskbar.app/task#5016d533-...)`. Paste it into a pull request, an issue, a comment or a message, and a click on it opens the task in its own window on the PC where Deskbar runs, on the desktop you are on.

- The link goes through a page on deskbar.app, because GitHub and most other places only keep web links. That page hands the link to Deskbar, so your browser asks once whether to open Deskbar; tick its "always allow" box and later links open straight away. The task's id stays in the part after `#`, which a browser never sends to the site.
- Deskbar registers the `deskbar:` link type for you when it starts, so a `deskbar://task/<id>` link opens the task too. If Deskbar is not running, the click starts it and then opens the task.
- A task link inside Deskbar, in a comment, a description or a question, opens the task's window directly, without the browser.
- Claude sessions on a task are told to put the task's link at the top of everything they make for it that you open outside Deskbar: pull requests, issues, published pages and mockups, documents and reports. Each of them leads back to its task.
- `deskbar.cmd task link <id>` prints the same markdown link.

## The Inbox

**Inbox**, first in the row at the bottom of the panel, shows the same tasks as the List in tabs, with a task opened as a page inside the panel, the way a chat app opens a chat. That row is the panel's foot in every view: **Inbox**, **List**, **Matrix** (from 780 DIP) and **Stats**, the view on show on a pill, and the **Manager** button at its right end. The List keeps its own List, Matrix and Stats buttons at the top as well. Nothing is taken away: List, Matrix and Stats are one click away and look as before. The panel remembers the Inbox once you pick it, and **List** or **Matrix** leave it. While the Inbox shows, the panel is a darker blue-black and the toolbar's rows give way to the Inbox's own header:

- **Tasks** with **+ New** (the same add box) and the x that closes the panel. The search box sits under the header at all times, so you can click into it and type (Ctrl+F puts the keyboard there too). A task's page hides it while it is empty; Ctrl+F brings it back there.
- Three buttons, each with its count: **Needs you** (the questions, the tasks handed back because their session went quiet, then the finished ones), **Claude** (working now, then idle under a dashed line, then the ones stopped on the usage limit) and **Notes** (the four quadrants as **Do first**, **Plan**, **Quick ones** and **Someday**, each with Covey's words after it). Every task the List shows is in exactly one of them. A subtask stays under its parent. A fourth button, **All**, at the end of the row, lists every task of the three together: Needs you's first, then Claude's, then the notes, each under its usual captions, and its count is the three counts added up.
- **This workspace | Everywhere**, the same switch as the **Current workspace** box, kept across restarts. **Filter** at the right folds out a sheet with all the List's filters (status, the Claude switch, Subtasks, the workspace, assignee and goal menus), and says how many are on ("Filter · 2"). It stays open until you click **Filter** again.

While the search box has text, or **Closed** or **Snoozed** is the status filter, the tabs step aside and the Inbox lists the matches in one list, with the tab buttons dimmed. A task's id or a pull request's number finds it as in the List.

Each line has the task's state as a shape in a rounded square: a speech bubble with "?" when it asks you, two branches joining into one when all it asks is that you merge a pull request (its question opens with "Merge" or "Review and merge", links the pull request on GitHub, asks nothing else (no question mark) and offers no answer buttons; the bar's lines in this look show it too), an amber ring open at the top right with a dot in the gap when its change is merged and only your deploy is left (a session hands that over with `task ask - --deploy`, and Deskbar also reads a merged task whose question offers no answer buttons that way; the session's deploy report settles it), a triangle with "!" when it went quiet or could not be restarted, a filled disc with a tick when it is finished, a ring with a tick when it is finished but its change is not running here yet, a dashed ring with a tick when it is finished but its session cannot check the deploy (its line then says why, such as "Can't check the deploy: no access to the Portainer logs"), a turning arc while Claude works, a pause sign when its session is idle or ended, a clock when it is stopped on the usage limit, and an empty box for a note no session ever worked on. A note's box closes it, with the usual six seconds of Undo, and so does a finished task's tick. On any other Claude task a click on the mark is **Reevaluate**: the session wakes, checks where the task really stands and follows it up: it rebases a pull request that fell behind or conflicts, fixes a failed check, answers review comments, and fixes what is stale, such as a question already answered or an old status line. Then come the title (two rows at most, bold when it needs you or is important, italic when urgent, the whole title on the task's page), the workspace chip (click it to move the task), one line of text (the question, else the status line, else the first line of the description), and at the right how long the state has lasted ("34m", "2h3m", amber when it needs you) with small marks under it: important, urgent, a pull request, the subtasks, a link and the comments. Point at a line for its buttons: **Open Claude** (or **Start Claude on this**), **Reevaluate**, **Open in window**, **Snooze**, **Close task** (on a task Claude still works on it offers **Stop Claude and close** first) and the ⋯ menu, the List's whole menu, which a right-click opens too. Double-click a line to go to its workspace and session. Lines drag as in the List: within a quadrant to reorder, onto another quadrant's lines to take its flags, onto a line's middle to make a subtask. F2 edits the selected line's title and description in place.

Click a line to open its page. The page opens with the keyboard already in its comment box, so you can start typing at once, and the manager's page (the **Manager** button) does the same. Esc in the empty box goes back; with words in it, Esc leaves them alone and the page stays. The manager's page opens in a panel of its own, docked beside the tasks panel, where Esc closes the panel as its × does, under the same rule: not while the box holds words. The top row has the back arrow (Esc does the same), the state mark, the title and the ⋯ menu; under them the state in words ("Waits for your answer", "Waits for your merge", "Merged, built · waiting for your deploy" with a Merged, Built, Deploy, Running track, "Finished. Can't check the deploy" with a Merged, Built, "Deploy: can't check" track and the session's reason under it, "Claude is working", "Finished. Check it and close"), the workspace chip and the task's pull requests as links. On a Claude task the state mark and the state words are **Reevaluate** too: click either to have the session check the task again. Then the description in full with its pictures, the question in a bubble with an amber edge while the task waits (or the status line, its links clickable), the subtasks, and the task's whole thread: its buttons, the sessions, the links, the comments as a chat, and the comment box, which takes pictures as in the List. The page's chat is whole: every comment stays in its place, none folded behind "N earlier comments", and a settled comment, such as the ones a session settles with `task answered`, is dimmed where it is rather than folded away. A short page sits at the bottom, as a chat does: the newest message just above the comment box at the bottom edge, the empty room between the header and the chat. A long one opens at its end and follows the newest message. The comment box stays at the bottom edge while the chat scrolls, and **Open Claude** sits at its left, so the session is one click away however long the chat is; a task with no session to open has **Start Claude on this** there instead, and the row of buttons at the top of the thread leaves the Claude button out. A finished task's page ends with **Close task** and **Not done yet…**, which puts the keyboard in the comment box. A subtask opens its own page, and back returns to its parent's. The page stays until you leave it: back, Esc, another task, closing the task or another view. The manager's question line stays above it.

**Answer buttons.** When the question a task waits on came with answers (a session's `--options`, at most four), the page draws them as buttons right under the question, in the chat or in the amber bubble, whichever shows it. One click posts that answer's exact words as your comment, as typing it would, and the session reads it the same way. Tab reaches each button and Enter or Space presses the focused one. A click on buttons that a newer question has replaced is refused with one line under the buttons instead of answering the wrong question; the line goes away when the question or its answers change. A List line's thread keeps the question as text.

**Claude's messages and steps.** On the page of a task that has a session, the chat also shows what that session said and did, read from its transcript only while the page is open: its messages, and between them each run of steps (read a file, ran a command, edited a file) folded as "N steps", which unfolds to one row per step. A step that failed says "failed" with its own mark; one still running says "running"; one that never got a result says "no result". Deskbar's own calls to its task commands are left out, and anything that looks like a secret in a command is masked. Deskbar never starts, stops or writes to the session for this.

**What moved into the ⋯ menu.** The Inbox's lines carry fewer buttons than the List's. **Edit**, **Add subtask**, **Assign to**, **Related to**, **Move to**, **Delete** and **Reopen** are now on the ⋯ menu only (and the page's), one click deeper than on a List line; the line keeps the mark, the title, the workspace chip and, while the pointer is on it, Open Claude or Start Claude. The task's status (Open, In progress, Backlog) is no longer written on a line: it shows as the tab the task sits in, the ticked item of **⋯ > Status**, and the **Filter** sheet. The assignee is the ticked item of **⋯ > Assign to** and the **Assigned** filter. The count of sessions is not on the line; the page lists the task's sessions. The age is replaced by how long the task has been in its state. In a task's own window, **⋯** also has **Go to its workspace** for a task with no session.


A click on a task on the bar, a task in Stats, a task link or **Open parent** switches to the task's tab and opens its page (on **All** the tab stays, since All holds every task). That page takes the place of the one that was open, so back goes to the list, not to the page before it; only a subtask opened from its parent's page goes back to the parent (and **Open parent** from a subtask's page goes back to that parent's page). A Claude task's terminal coming to the front selects its task as in the List: its line lights up and scrolls into view, and when it is in another tab than the one on show, the panel switches to the tab that holds it (on **All** it stays on All). While a task's page is open, the page stays: the selection moves underneath it, as an open thread in the List stays open.

A task you add from the panel (**+ New**) shows in its tab: the tab switches to it, and its line glows and comes into view, unless a page is open, which then stays as it is. The panel follows that task once more: when Claude starts on it (**Start Claude on this** later on the same selected line), the panel moves to the **Claude** tab with it. Enter or the play button in the add box starts Claude in the background instead and leaves the panel where it is; Ctrl+Enter only adds. Picking a tab, opening a page, searching or changing a filter, or selecting another task ends that. A task added from the bar's box or by a session leaves the panel's tab alone.

The Inbox's first version leaves out the full-width table the List draws from 850 DIP: its lines are the same at every width.

**The bar's lines follow the panel's look.** While the panel shows the Inbox, the task list on the bar draws its lines the same way: the state mark, the title on up to two lines (semibold on a Claude task), and the workspace (a dot in its colour and its name) at the right end of the title's first row, so a task with a short title takes one row. A long workspace name ends in "…" and is whole in its tooltip; a click on it moves the task to another workspace, and a task on no workspace has none. That is all a line shows: its question or status line, and a permission question its session waits on, are in its tooltip, in full, with the title. There is no orange: only a mark that needs you is amber. The first line of the bar's waiting block carries a small **NEEDS YOU** caption with the block's count, the lines folded into "+N more waiting on you" included. That block is the questions and the finished tasks; a task handed back because its session went quiet stays among the bar's other lines, so the bar's count can be lower than the panel's **Needs you**. The add box is rounded, with a thin blue edge while you type in it, and the row under it holds only what belongs to adding: the workspace chip (an outline with a dot in the workspace's colour and its full name), the Important and Urgent toggles (filled while on), the camera after them and the play button at the right end. The panel button and **Manager** move to a row at the end of the list: **All tasks**, followed by "· 3 more" when lines are out of sight on the bar, or **Hide tasks** while the panel is open, and **Manager**, with its dot when Deskbar woke the manager. What the bar lists, and every click, drag and menu on it, is the same in both looks. With the panel on List or Matrix the bar keeps its old look.

## Stats

**Stats**, after **List** and **Matrix** at the top right of the tasks panel, shows how the work is going. It is there at every width. While Stats is on, **List** shows too, even on a narrow panel, as the way back. **Matrix** goes back as well where the panel is wide enough for it. The search box and the filter chips step aside while Stats shows, because Stats counts every task, whatever the filters say. The panel opens on the List or the Matrix again the next time, so Stats is not remembered.

- Four numbers at the top: **Open** (backlog, in progress and waiting on you), **Added, 7 days**, **Finished, 7 days** and **Median to finish**, the middle time from added to finished over the tasks finished in the last 14 days. Finished means Done or closed.
- **Open at the end of the day**: one line, one point a day, today at the right with its number. This is what a burndown would draw. A burndown assumes a fixed amount of work shrinking to zero, though, and a task list keeps growing, so the chart below shows why the line moved.
- **Added and finished per day**: two bars a day, blue for added and green for finished.
- The charts start on the day your oldest task was added and cover between 7 and 14 days. Point at a day's column for its three numbers.
- Under the charts, the picked day's tasks: **Finished** and **Added**, each line the task's status and title. It is today until you click another day's column. Click a task to go to it: the panel leaves Stats and shows the task with its comments open. For a closed task it switches to the **Closed** chip, and any workspace, assignee or goal filter that would hide the task goes back to all.

From this version on, Deskbar records when each task's status changes, so finish times are exact. For tasks finished before that, the finish time is estimated: when a done task became done, and when a closed task was last changed (closing changes it). While any counted task leans on an estimate, a line under the charts says so. A task reopened and finished again counts once, on its last finish.

## Subtasks

A task can have subtasks, one level deep: a subtask cannot have subtasks of its own.

- Pick **Add subtask…** on a line's menu, on the bar or in the tasks panel (after the Claude item). The add box opens with the parent as a chip, "↳ <parent>", next to the workspace chip, which moves to the parent's workspace. Type a title and press Enter. Esc, or a click on the parent chip, drops the parent and leaves what you typed as a plain note. On a subtask's line, **Add subtask…** adds a sibling under the same parent.
- **Drag a task onto the middle of another** to make it that task's subtask: while the pointer is over the middle half of a line, the line is outlined instead of a drop line showing between two. The top and bottom quarters of a line reorder as before. A subtask, or any line while you drag a task that has subtasks of its own, takes no child, so there a drop only reorders. Tasks waiting on you or done nest and take subtasks like any other. Drag a subtask out of its parent's block and drop it between other lines to make it a plain task again. This works on the bar and in the tasks panel's List; in the Matrix a drag still only moves a task between quadrants.
- A subtask is drawn right under its parent, in the parent's group, whatever its own flags, and starts with "↳". A parent waiting on you or done takes its subtasks up with it, under it at the top. A subtask that waits on you or is done, under a parent that is neither, stays at the top with the others of its kind, and in the Matrix each task stays in its own quadrant. When its parent is not in the list (filtered out in the tasks panel, or a Claude task the bar does not show), the subtask stays in its own place, and in the tasks panel's full line the parent's name follows the arrow, as in "↳ Ship the release › Write the notes"; the bar's tooltip names the parent. A parent's line says how many open subtasks it has, as in "Ship the release · 2 subtasks".
- Untick **Subtasks** at the top of the tasks panel to fold subtasks into their parent, in the panel and on the bar: the parent's line still says how many it has, and opening it lists them. Only a subtask whose parent is on the list folds, so one whose parent is filtered out still shows, and a subtask waiting on you stays at the top with the other questions. In the Matrix it folds a subtask whose parent is in any cell. Like **Current workspace**, the box stays as you left it when the panel closes and after a restart.
- Open a parent in the tasks panel and its thread lists its subtasks, each with its status and status line; click one to go to it. **Add subtask**, in the row of buttons at the top of the thread, does what the menu item does, with the box opening right under that row.
- An open thread in the tasks panel starts with one row of buttons: **Open in window**, then **Open Claude** on a Claude task, then **Start Claude on this** while no session of the task is running (a Claude task whose sessions have all ended shows both), then **Add subtask**. A task with a single session lists it under the row without an Open of its own, since **Open Claude** does the same; with two or more, each session line keeps its Open. A button the task's line already shows (see [buttons on a line](#buttons-on-a-line)) is left out of the row, and at the default width that is all of them, so the row is there only on a narrow panel.
- Closing a parent leaves its subtasks as they are. Deleting a parent makes its subtasks plain tasks.
- A parent with open subtasks will not close. In the tasks panel the reason, "Close its open subtask first", shows in orange under the parent's title, and the same goes for any other action the panel refuses on a task: the reason sits on that task's line and in its own window, and goes once your next action works.
- What a subtask is for: a Claude session that takes one up is told about the family, so the title can be all you write. See [With Claude sessions](#with-claude-sessions).

## Duplicates and related tasks

- Pick **Duplicate of…** on a line's menu in the tasks panel, type part of the other task's title and pick it from the short list under the box (Enter picks the first one, Esc closes the box). The task closes as a duplicate: it leaves the open lists like any closed task, and under **Closed** it says "Duplicate of <title>". A duplicate of a duplicate points at the original. The rules of closing hold: a parent with open subtasks, or a task Claude is still working on, is refused with the same reason its tick gives. Reopen it and it is a plain task again.
- Pick **Related to…** the same way to link two tasks. The link shows in both tasks' threads.
- Open a task in the tasks panel and its thread lists its links above the comments: "Duplicate of", "Duplicate" (a task closed as a duplicate of this one) and "Related", each with its status. Click one to go to it; a closed one is shown under **Closed**. A related link has a cross that removes it.
- What links are for: a Claude session on a task reads the linked tasks and their sessions' conversations before it starts, and looks for duplicates itself. See [With Claude sessions](#with-claude-sessions).

## Recurring tasks

A task can repeat, for work you want done again at a set time: a morning summary, an hourly check of a build. A Claude session does each run, so setting a repeat gives the task to Claude.

- Pick **Repeat** on a line's "⋯" menu, in the tasks panel or in the task window. It offers "Every hour", "Every hour, 09:00-19:00", "Every day at 09:00", "Every weekday at 09:00", "Every Monday at 09:00", **Custom…** and, while the task repeats, **Off**. The current schedule is ticked.
- **Custom…** asks for the same words `task repeat` takes (see [From a script](#from-a-script)), such as `every 30m`, `weekdays 08:30` or `weekly friday 16:00`, and `--model` is allowed there. Times are in this PC's time. An interval can be from 15 minutes to 24 hours, and a window after it, such as `09:00-19:00`, stays within one day.
- A run uses Sonnet unless you name another model. It runs in the task's folder: the only one Deskbar knows for it, or the one you pick from a list when there are several. A worktree counts as its repo. Changing the schedule later keeps the model and the folder, unless the folder has gone.
- A task that repeats shows a repeat mark on its line. Hover it to read "Repeats every hour · next run Thu 09:00 · sonnet". Between runs the task waits under the panel's **Snoozed** chip, and **Wake now** starts a run at once.
- A run is quiet. It opens no window and takes no focus, and the task does not jump to the top. It comments on the task and marks it done, and the task goes back to waiting for the next time. About a minute after a run goes idle Deskbar stops its session, unless you have it open in a window.
- A run that comes due while the last one is still going is skipped, not queued. There is no catch-up: if the PC was asleep or off at the time, the run is simply missed and the next one comes at its next time.
- A comment you post between runs starts a fresh run that reads it, but only once the last run's session has been stopped, about a minute after it goes idle. Before that, the comment goes to that session, as a comment on any task does.
- If the folder is gone, or a run cannot start, the task asks you once, and then keeps quiet rather than asking at every time.
- **Off** between runs, or while the task asks you about its folder or a run that could not start, ends the job: the task becomes done, and you close it as you would any finished task. **Off** while a run is going only drops the schedule and lets that run finish. Closing a repeating task stops its repeats, and reopening it starts them again from that moment.
- A task's own Claude session may set its task's repeat or turn it off. The manager may set a repeat on any task by its id, but it may not turn one off.

## Tasks from older versions

Earlier versions had a stopwatch that parked a workspace for a few minutes. That is gone. Tasks from those versions come back as notes, with their timers dropped, and the old file is kept beside the new one as `tasks.json.v1`. That copy appears the first time the tasks change (you add, tick or close one, or a script does), not at start. Until then `tasks.json` is still the old file.

If `tasks.json` cannot be read at all, the list says so on its first line and keeps the unreadable file aside as a `.bak`. The cross at the end of that line dismisses the message.

## Settings

Untick **Task list** under the strip items in Manage's settings to hide the list. Tasks are kept in `tasks.json`, beside `state.json` in `%APPDATA%\Deskbar`.

## From a script

```powershell
$ts = "$env:APPDATA\Deskbar\deskbar.cmd"
& $ts task add "Wait for CI"                # a note on the caller's own workspace
& $ts task add "Call the bank" --workspace none
'Buy "the good" soap & tablets' | & $ts task add -   # a title with quotes or &: - reads it from stdin
& $ts task add "Renew the passport" --important --urgent   # quadrant I, at its top
& $ts task list                             # JSON: the open tasks in the bar's order
& $ts task list --closed                    # the closed ones, the latest closed first
& $ts task reopen 3f2a                      # bring a closed task back
& $ts task close 3f2a                       # an id, or a unique prefix of four or more characters
& $ts task flag 3f2a important on           # or urgent; off clears it
& $ts task repeat 3f2a "every 1h"           # make it recurring; "daily 09:00", "weekdays 09:00", "weekly monday 09:00"
& $ts task repeat 3f2a off                   # end the job
& $ts task order 3f2a top                   # or end, or a place in its quadrant counting from 1
& $ts tasks-window matrix                   # or list, or inbox
```

`task list` gives each task's `important` and `urgent` flags, its `quadrant` ("I" to "IV") and its `order`, its place in that quadrant counting from 1 (null while it waits on you or is done, since it is pinned on top until you answer or close it).

The full set:

- `task list [--closed]`
- `task overview`, one line per open task (id, status, flags, workspace, title, status line, question, and whether a Claude is working, idle or done), with subtasks indented; plain text, cheaper to read than `task list`
- `task show <id>`, one task in full, thread included, as `task list` prints it
- `task manager`, the **Manager** button's twin: opens today's [manager session](claude-sessions.md#the-manager-session), starting or resuming it when it is not running
- `task add <title>|- [--workspace <name>|none] [--important] [--urgent]`, with `-` reading the title from stdin; the flags put the note at the top of the quadrant they give
- `task close <id>` and `task reopen <id>`. A task Claude is still working on refuses a plain `task close` and says so; `task close <id> --stop-claude` closes it and stops its background sessions, as **Stop Claude and close** does. A task that still asks something or has a live session, and whose work is not merged, refuses too; `task close <id> --anyway` closes it, as **Close anyway** does.
- `task status <id> <backlog|in-progress|waiting|done>`
- `task rename <id> <title>|-`, with `-` reading the title from stdin
- `task move <id> <workspace>|none`
- `task comment <id> <markdown>`
- `task delete <id>`
- `task flag <id> important|urgent on|off`, the toggles' twin; the task moves to the top of the quadrant it lands in
- `task order <id> top|end|<n>`, the drag's twin, within the task's own quadrant; a task waiting on you or done moves among the questions or the done tasks on top
- `tasks-window show` and `tasks-window hide`, to open or bring forward the tasks panel, or close it
- `tasks-window inbox`, `tasks-window list` and `tasks-window matrix`, to switch the panel's view (remembered); list and matrix leave the [Inbox](#the-inbox)
- `tasks-window stats`, to open the panel on its [Stats](#stats) view (not remembered)
- `task stats [--days <n>]`, the Stats view's numbers as JSON: `openNow`, `addedLast7Days`, `finishedLast7Days`, `medianHoursToFinish` (null when nothing finished), `estimated`, and `days`, one per local day (14 by default, today last), each with its `date`, `openAtEnd`, and the tasks `added` and `finished` that day (`id`, `title`, `status`)
- `task assign <id> me|claude`, the **Assign to** menu's twin; `task list` gives each task's `assignee`, `me` or `claude`
- `task comment-settle <comment-id> --session <id>`, a session settling one of its own comments (another's is refused); `task list`'s `thread` shows each comment's `settled` time, or null
- `task describe <id> <markdown>|-`, the description Edit sets (empty clears it), and `task comment-edit <comment-id> <markdown>|-`, which rewrites one of your own comments and refuses a session's; `task list` gives each task's `description` and its `thread`, each comment's id, `you` or `claude` for who wrote it, and its `markdown`, the comment's text; in both, a pasted picture's line carries its full path, ready for a session to read
- `task open <id>`, the menus' **Open in window**: the task in a window of its own, or its window brought forward onto the desktop you are on
- `task link <id>`, the menus' **Copy link**: the markdown link that opens the task in Deskbar when clicked (see [Links to a task](#links-to-a-task))
- `task goal <id> <goal-id>|none`, the **Goal** menu's twin; `task list` gives each task's `goal` (see [Goals](goals.md))
- `task add <title> --parent <id>` adds a subtask; without `--workspace` it goes on the parent's workspace
- `task parent <id> <parent-id>|none` puts a task under another, or makes it a plain task again; a second level is refused
- `task family <id>` prints what a Claude session on the task reads about its family and its linked tasks (plain text); `task list` gives each task's `parent` (`{id, title}` or null) and `subtasks` (ids)
- `task duplicate <id> <of-id>` closes a task as a duplicate of another, **Duplicate of…**'s twin; `task relate <id> <other-id>` and `task unrelate <id> <other-id>` add and remove a related link; `task list` gives each task's `duplicateOf` (`{id, title}` or null; its `status` stays `closed`), `duplicates` (ids) and `related` (ids, both ways)

A Claude Code session's own forms, each with `--session <id>` (see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md)):

- `task join <id>` and `task new <title>`, to take up a task or create one
- `task status-line <text>`, `task ask <question>` (the text may end in a line `--options` followed by up to 4 short answers, one per line, which the task keeps beside the question; see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md)), `task comment <markdown>`, `task answered` (its question is settled: back to in progress, the blink stops; nothing happens when the task is not waiting) and `task done`, on the session's own task
- `task later <title>`, a note for later on the session's workspace, linked to the session's own task as related so it says where it came from (`task add ... --session <id>` does the same with `add`'s flags)
- `task subtask <title> [--no-start] [--model <name>]`, a subtask of the session's own task (a sibling when that is a subtask), assigned to Claude, with a background session started on it at once; `--no-start` leaves it for the next session to be offered
- `task family`, its own task's family and linked tasks, as its `task join` reply gave them
- `task relate <id>` and `task unrelate <id>`, a related link from its own task; `task duplicate-of <id>`, its own task closes as a duplicate of another (the one way a session closes a task)

Every form that takes text, `task add <title>` and `task comment <id> <markdown>` included, takes `-` in the text's place and reads the text from stdin. Use it for any text with quotes or `& | < >` in it: `deskbar.cmd` hands its command line to cmd, which could run part of such a text as a command.

## With Claude sessions

Every Claude Code session keeps a task in this list. When it starts, it is offered the open tasks on its workspace, joins the one its work continues or creates its own, and keeps a short status line under the title. A task a session works on is Claude's: it has an orange edge (and no icon: the title starts at the line's left edge, as on a note), and a session that joins one of your notes makes it Claude's work.

When a session needs a decision from you, its task moves to the top as **Waiting on you**, with the question on the line, and the workspace blinks. Your message alone does not answer it: the session settles the question itself with `task answered`, once your reply settles it, and the task goes back to in progress. **The bar lists only the tasks assigned to you.** Every task has an assignee, you or Claude. A task you create is yours. A session's task is Claude's while it works, becomes yours when the session asks you something or marks it done, and goes back to Claude when the session settles the question with `task answered` or takes up more work on it after it was done. So a Claude task is on the bar because it is assigned to you, which follows from the session (and from the status you set by hand: Waiting on you and Done assign it to you, and reopening a task works its assignee out again), and once the session has settled the question it leaves the bar (it stays in the tasks panel, which shows every task), and it comes back at the top when the session asks again. Nothing else takes it off: going to its workspace, switching to the session's window, or clicking or double-clicking the line leaves it there. You can also hand any task over yourself in the tasks panel, with **Assign to** in its menu: a note you give to Claude leaves the bar, and is offered to a new session starting on its workspace; a task you give back to yourself stays on the bar. You assign only in the tasks panel, never on the bar. A task that is waiting on you and that you hand to Claude leaves the bar and its "+N waiting" count, but stays Waiting on you in the tasks panel with its question, and its workspace keeps blinking until you visit it or the session answers. "+N more" counts only what the bar lists. A session marks its task done when it has finished; the line keeps its place and waits for you to close it, because only you close a task. A message to a session whose task is done leaves it done (it may only ask about the finished work); when the message gives the session more work on it, the session puts it back in progress itself with `task join`. A session whose task you closed is told so on its next message: if the message carries on with that work, the session reopens the task with `task join`, and only other work gets a new task. Resuming a session whose task you closed reopens the task straight away, in progress and back at the top of its quadrant, so you can carry on with it.

A session that joins a subtask is told about its family in the reply to `task join`: the parent's line, your description and its last comments, and the other subtasks, each with its Claude sessions, their folders and the file each session's conversation is kept in. It is asked to read the end of the parent session's conversation before it starts, and to stay clear of what the other subtasks are doing. A session on a parent is told about its subtasks the same way. The family comes again when a session resumes, and `task family` prints it on demand.

Before a session starts on a task, it looks through the open and closed tasks for the same or related work and reads what their sessions did. It links a related task with `task relate`, and when its task is work already done or under way elsewhere, it closes its task as a duplicate with `task duplicate-of` instead of doing the work again; reopen it if it got that wrong. The reply to `task join` lists the linked tasks (as `linked`) with their sessions and the files their conversations are kept in, the same way the family is listed.

A task whose sessions have all ended stays open and says so. Sessions nobody is sitting at (`claude -p`, Agent SDK runs) make no tasks. The setting is **Claude Code sessions keep their tasks in the task list** in Manage; see [Claude Code sessions](claude-sessions.md).
